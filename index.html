What We Do

Services Built Around Real Work

Six areas of work. Each one states what we deliver, the problem it solves and who it is for.

01

Web Systems

What we deliver
Business websites, customer portals, dashboards and web applications — with the database and APIs behind them.
Problem it solves
Work that lives in spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads and paper forms, with no single place to record, find or report on it.
Who benefits
Small and medium-sized businesses, institutions and teams that need one reliable system instead of disconnected tools.
02

Software & Desktop Applications

What we deliver
Desktop applications and internal tools for Windows, macOS and Linux — including offline data entry, file processing, reporting and system utilities.
Problem it solves
Repetitive manual work, fragile spreadsheets and tools that stop working when the internet drops.
Who benefits
Offices, institutions and operations teams that depend on local machines and need software that runs on them.
03

Mobile Applications

What we deliver
Android and iOS applications, progressive web apps and mobile-first interfaces, including offline storage and low-data behaviour.
Problem it solves
Services designed for fast, unlimited internet that become unusable on real handsets, slow networks and expensive data.
Who benefits
Businesses and organisations reaching customers or staff in low-connectivity environments, and individuals who need a dependable mobile tool.
04

AI & Business Automation

What we deliver
Workflow automation, document and data processing, reporting assistants and AI features applied to a defined task.
Problem it solves
Time lost to repetitive admin, slow reporting and decisions made without timely information.
Who benefits
Teams with high-volume routine work that want automation where it measurably saves time — not AI for its own sake.
05

Enterprise & Digital Identity Systems

What we deliver
Identity and access systems, attendance and verification workflows, role-based access and audit trails, integrated with existing records.
Problem it solves
Identity, attendance and access stored in separate places, with no consistent way to confirm who did what, when and where.
Who benefits
Institutions, campuses and organisations that need accountable records and controlled access.
06

Networking & Emerging Technologies

What we deliver
Network design and setup, connectivity planning, offline and edge infrastructure, device integration and early-stage technology prototypes.
Problem it solves
Unreliable connectivity, weak network visibility and systems that assume infrastructure many sites do not have.
Who benefits
Schools, offices, campuses and sites where bandwidth, power and reliability cannot be taken for granted.

Every engagement starts with the problem — not a preset package. If we are not the right fit, we will say so.

Innovation Lab

Research, Experiments & What Comes Next

Not everything here is a product. Lab work is grouped by how far it has actually progressed — research questions, active experiments and future directions.

Current research

Open questions under investigation. No build has been committed.

  • On-device AI for low-end handsets Running useful models within limited memory, storage and power — and knowing when to defer to the cloud.
  • Offline data reconciliation Resolving conflicts when several devices edit the same records without a connection.
  • Identity without special hardware Linking a credential to a person, device, time and place using tools most institutions already own.
Active experiments

Work with code behind it, still short of a released product.

  • Neo SmartCore — operations core Prototype. Testing stock thresholds, daily summaries and exception handling against real business records.
  • FeMOS — offline campus attendance In development. Rotating check-in codes, geofencing and queued sync on campus networks.
  • FastLine Identifying Tool — identity layer Concept. A reusable credential and verification model for attendance and access.
Future directions

Themes worth pursuing once the current work is solid.

  • Device-to-device networking Local data exchange where mobile networks are absent, congested or too expensive.
  • Edge infrastructure for institutions Campus and site servers that keep core services running through an internet outage.
  • Automation for small operations Routine reporting and stock control applied where the time saved can be measured.

Research themes are not products. They move into active experiments only when there is a defined problem and a way to test the answer.

How I Work

Five principles behind every build.

These are not slogans. They are the checks applied before a project is accepted, while it is built and after it is handed over.

  1. 01

    Solve the real problem first.

    Start with the work people actually do, not the technology we want to use.

  2. 02

    Design for the conditions that exist.

    Bandwidth, devices, budgets and environments are part of the brief.

  3. 03

    Build systems that keep working.

    Reliability is a feature; offline resilience is designed in, not added later.

  4. 04

    Be honest about stage.

    Concept, prototype and released product are different things and are labelled as such.

  5. 05

    Keep improving after launch.

    A system earns its place by continuing to solve the problem it was built for.
